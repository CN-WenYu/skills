# Evidence Research

First establish the research object, decision purpose, time cutoff, and evidence standard. Separate observable facts, inferences grounded in facts, and value judgments. Cite sources and dates for important conclusions, and state evidence gaps plainly.

## Use The Relevant Method

- **Fact-checking:** Split a claim into verifiable facts, inferences, and value judgments. Check the source, sample, timing, and full context. Label findings as verified, broadly true but needing qualification, disputed, insufficiently evidenced, or clearly false.
- **Longitudinal and comparative analysis:** Track origin, important turning points, and path dependencies over time. Compare suitable subjects on shared dimensions, explain adoption and rejection, then state conditional future paths and their warning signals.
- **Cross-domain transfer:** Abstract the underlying structure and core tension before choosing cases that are genuinely isomorphic rather than superficially similar. Explain the other domain's mechanism, transferable parts, failure conditions, and a reversible test in the current context.

## Output

Provide a core conclusion, an evidence table or source list, unresolved questions, and recommendations tied to the research purpose. Do not turn unverified information into certainty or correlation into causation.
